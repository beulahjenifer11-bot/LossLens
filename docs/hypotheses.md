## Step 15, written before running
Setup: rate 2 Mbps, one-way delay 20 ms, buffer 10 packets, fixed window 20, no random loss.
Prediction: BDP = 2 Mbps x 40 ms = 10,000 bytes, about 8 packets. Pipe 8 + buffer 10 = 18 < window 20, so the queue must overflow, max queue depth will reach 10, srtt will rise well above the 40 ms min RTT, and goodput will be capped near 1.9 Mbps.

### Step 15 outcome
All predictions held: max queue depth 10 of 10, srtt 87.3 ms vs min RTT 45.5 ms, goodput 1.90 Mbps, 49 QUEUE_OVERFLOW drops. Min RTT was above 40 ms because of 4.9 ms serialization delay at 2 Mbps, which I had not included in the prediction.

## Step 18 (scenario S2 random loss sweep), written before running
Setup: 20 Mbps, 40 ms RTT, buffer 80 packets, 5 MB file, controllers reno and fixed window of 20, loss 0, 0.1, 0.5, 1, 2, 5 percent.
Mathis estimates for Reno at 1200 byte MSS and 40 ms RTT: 9.3 Mbps at 0.1 percent, 4.1 at 0.5, 2.9 at 1, 2.1 at 2, 1.3 at 5.
Prediction 1 (H1): Reno goodput falls steeply with loss and is below 5 Mbps (25 percent of capacity) at 1 percent loss, within a factor of 2 of the Mathis estimates.
Prediction 2: the fixed window is capped near 4.6 Mbps at 0 percent loss and falls only mildly with loss, so Reno wins at 0 and 0.1 percent loss, the fixed window wins from 1 percent upward, and the crossover lies between 0.1 and 1 percent.
Prediction 3: every transfer passes the hash check, and losses declared match the emulator drop count with spurious near 0.

### Step 18 outcome (full run, 120 runs, seeds 1 to 10)
Prediction 1 supported: Reno at 1 percent loss reached 2.82 Mbps (95 percent CI 2.65 to 2.99), 14.1 percent of capacity, below the 25 percent limit. Reno was within a factor of 2 of the Mathis estimate at every loss rate (ratio 1.18, 1.02, 0.96, 0.91, 0.85 at 0.1, 0.5, 1, 2, 5 percent). Ratio below 1 at higher loss is probably from timeouts, which Mathis ignores, and above 1 at 0.1 percent from the slow start share of a 5 MB run.
Prediction 2 mostly supported: the fixed window stayed between 4.37 and 4.66 Mbps at all loss rates, and Reno won at 0 and 0.1 percent (18.26 and 10.97 Mbps against 4.65 and 4.66). The crossover is earlier than predicted: at 0.5 percent the fixed window was already ahead (4.64 against 4.24, intervals do not overlap), so it lies between 0.1 and 0.5 percent, not at 1 percent.
Prediction 3: spurious losses were 0.00 in all 12 settings (n=10 each). Hash and drop count checks to be confirmed from the script output.
Unexpected finding: Reno retransmitted 3.58 percent of packets at 0 percent random loss and 3.00 percent at 0.1 percent, so at low random loss most of its losses are congestion drops from slow start overshoot (no pacing yet). The fixed window retransmission rate matched the random loss rate (0.10, 0.53, 1.07, 2.05, 5.11 percent) because 20 packets never overflow the buffer.

## Step 23 (CUBIC in scenario S2), written before running
Prediction 4: Reno is AIMD with increase 1 packet per RTT and beta 0.5, giving a throughput constant sqrt(1 x 1.5 / 1) = 1.22. CUBIC in the TCP-friendly region increases 0.53 packets per RTT with beta 0.7, giving sqrt(0.529 x 1.7 / 0.6) = 1.22, the same constant. Typical windows here are under about 40 packets, too small to leave the TCP-friendly region. So CUBIC goodput should be within 25 percent of Reno at every loss rate from 0.1 to 5 percent, and well below 25 percent of capacity at 1 percent loss (this extends Prediction 3 of Step 22 to all loss rates).
At 0 percent loss both should be near 18 Mbps, but start-up overshoot dominates, so a larger difference is possible. Retransmission rate at 0 percent loss should be similar to Reno (about 3.6 percent) or slightly higher because CUBIC keeps 70 percent after a cut.

### Step 23 outcome (CUBIC in S2, 10 seeds)
Prediction 4 partly supported: CUBIC goodput was within 25 percent of Reno at 5 of 6 loss rates (ratio 1.00, 1.06, 0.97, 0.93, 0.87 at 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 5 percent). It missed at 0.1 percent loss, where CUBIC reached about 14.1 Mbps against 10.97 for Reno (ratio 1.28) and the confidence intervals read from the graph do not overlap. Step 22 Prediction 3 held: at 1 percent loss CUBIC was 0.97 of Reno, about 2.74 Mbps.
My stated reason, that windows stay inside the TCP-friendly region, does not explain the 0.1 percent gap: by my calculation the TCP-friendly estimate stays above the cubic curve at these window sizes. Likely cause, not yet tested: a 5 MB run sees only about 5 cuts at 0.1 percent loss, so start-up matters, and CUBIC keeps 70 percent of cwnd after the slow start overshoot against 50 percent for Reno, a head start while cwnd is far below the BDP of 83 packets. A longer file at 0.1 percent loss would test this. CUBIC was 13 percent below Reno at 5 percent loss, cause not investigated.

### Step 23b outcome (diagnostic run, CUBIC at 0.1 percent loss, seeds 1 to 10)
Packet conservation held in all 10 runs: sent equals packets read by the emulator, and the receiver accepted exactly 4370 DATA packets (every chunk once). Declared losses equalled emulator drops in every run, spurious 0. The 83 extra declared losses seen in the full S2 run (CUBIC, 0.1 percent, seed 2) did not reproduce. The OS buffer theories (before the emulator, at the receiver) are not supported for these runs.
SACK truncation occurred on about 4000 of about 4370 ACKs in 8 of 10 runs (0 in the two runs with fewer than 50 drops). Cause: packet numbers are never reused, so every dropped packet number remains a hole, and once there are more than 50 holes every ACK is cut to its newest 50 ranges. Truncation alone cannot hide an arrival, because the ACK triggered by a packet always contains it. Remaining untested hypothesis: a lost ACK combined with truncation lets a delivered packet go unreported, so it is declared lost and retransmitted.
Observation: drops were bimodal. Eight runs had 221 to 239 drops (start-up overflow burst), seeds 3 and 10 had 6 and 26. Untested reading: an early random loss ended slow start before the overshoot in those two runs.
Prediction written before checking: seeds 3 and 10 have the highest goodput of the 10 runs.
Decision: documented as a limitation (3 of 180 runs, all CUBIC, 86 extra declared losses in total, every hash true). The analysis reports unlabelled declared losses as a separate bucket.

### Step 23b prediction check
Prediction that seeds 3 and 10 have the highest goodput was WRONG. Seed 3 was the slowest (10.40 Mbps, 4.03 s, 6 drops, 5 cuts), seed 10 was mid-pack (15.12 Mbps, 26 drops, 4 cuts), and the fastest was seed 2 (18.30 Mbps, 226 drops, 4 cuts). Goodput followed the number of cwnd reductions more than the number of drops: 4 cuts gave 15.12 and 18.30 Mbps, 5 cuts 16.87 (seed 5) and 10.40 (seed 3, the exception), 6 cuts 14.75 and 14.68, 7 cuts 12.74, 13.47 and 13.41, 8 cuts 14.09. A start-up burst of 226 to 239 drops cost only 4 to 8 cuts because losses within one RTT count as one event. The earlier reading that early random losses let a run escape the burst is not supported. Untested reading for seed 3: six isolated drops cut a small window five times and CUBIC regrows slowly in its TCP-friendly region.

## Step 26 threshold tuning, written before running
Data: tuning seeds 101 to 110 only, 1 MB file, LossLens controller, 12 threshold pairs (q_frac 0.05, 0.1, 0.2 times slope 0.5, 1, 2, 4 ms), two scenarios with 1 percent random loss and 40 ms RTT: mixed (2 Mbps, buffer 10) and fast (20 Mbps, buffer 80).
Selection rule, fixed in advance: per scenario, balanced accuracy = (congestion recall + random recall) / 2 on counts pooled over the 10 seeds. The score of a pair is the mean of the two scenario values. The best pair wins, and pairs within 0.02 of the best are ranked by mean goodput. The winner is written to docs/frozen_thresholds.md before any further test-seed (1 to 10) LossLens run.
Prediction 6: raising the slope threshold at a fixed q_frac raises random recall and lowers congestion recall, a trade-off and not a free improvement.
Prediction 7: the best score exceeds the default pair (0.1, 1) on the same seeds by at most 0.10, and the best balanced accuracy in the mixed scenario stays below 0.85, because H2 itself expects failure when random loss coincides with queue build-up.
Prediction 8: with the best pair, LossLens goodput in the fast scenario stays above 5 Mbps on the 1 MB file (a loose floor, because the run is short).

### Step 27 outcome (threshold tuning, seeds 101 to 110, 1 MB file, 240 runs, all hashes true)
Prediction 6 held in 5 of 6 cases (3 q_frac values x 2 scenarios). The miss was the fast scenario at q_frac 0.05, where going from slope 0.5 to 4 ms moved congestion recall from 0.76 to 0.78 (up, not down) and random recall from 0.68 to 0.64 (down, not up). In the fast scenario the trade-off holds in direction at q_frac 0.1 and 0.2 but is small: over an 8 fold range of slope threshold the recalls moved by at most 0.05 (congestion recall 0.84 to 0.79 and 0.80 to 0.78, random recall 0.59 to 0.63 and 0.61 to 0.64). The slope threshold is a weak knob in the fast scenario.
Prediction 8 held: with the frozen pair, LossLens goodput in the fast scenario averaged 13.27 Mbps (minimum 9.81) against the 5 Mbps floor, on a 1 MB file, so start-up dominates and this is not comparable with the 5 MB results.
Prediction 7, mixed scenario clause held: the winner balanced accuracy in the mixed scenario was 0.61, under 0.85. The gain clause needs a recheck: the script printed winner minus default as -0.02, but Prediction 7 was worded for the best score, not the winner, and the winner was chosen by the goodput tie-break among pairs within 0.02 of the best. The default pair (0.1, 1) scored about 0.02 higher than the winner, so tuning did not beat the default on balanced accuracy.
In the fast scenario, at the lowest and highest slope for each q_frac (six pairs), random recall was 0.59 to 0.68 and congestion recall 0.76 to 0.84. H2 (at least 85 percent for isolated random loss) held in the flat queue test of Step 25 (98 percent) but not here, where LossLens itself builds the queue. Whether the controller shaping its own signal is the cause is not tested.
